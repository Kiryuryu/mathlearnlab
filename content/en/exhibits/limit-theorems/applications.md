## Applications of the Laws of Large Numbers

### 1. The Weather Forecaster's "60%"

The forecast says "60% chance of rain" — not that 60% of the region will get wet, but that under similar weather conditions, it rained on 60% of such days. This is the **frequentist** reading of probability: probability is the limit of long-run frequency.

### 2. The Central Limit Theorem in Quality Control

The diameters of screws off a factory line theoretically follow some distribution. But the **average diameter** of many screws is approximately normal — that is the central limit theorem at work. Quality engineers monitor the mean with control charts: as long as the average stays within $\mu \pm 3\sigma$, the process is judged to be in control.

### 3. Actuarial Science

An insurer prices its policies on **expected loss**. The law of large numbers guarantees that with enough policyholders, actual payouts converge to the expected value — and the company earns a stable profit.

### 4. Monte Carlo Simulation

Estimate the numerical answer to a hard problem by random sampling: generate a flood of random samples and take their average — the law of large numbers guarantees the result converges to the true value. From financial risk to physics simulations, Monte Carlo is the art of "beating randomness with randomness."

### 5. Confidence Intervals in Polling

A poll reports "support at 52% ± 3%." That ±3% comes straight from $\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}$: the larger the sample, the smaller the error. The central limit theorem gives statistical inference its reliable boundaries.

---

**Behind all these applications**: the law of large numbers and the central limit theorem bridge "random" and "certain" — letting science project from sample to population.
