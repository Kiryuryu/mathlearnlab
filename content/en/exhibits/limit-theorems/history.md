## The Intellectual Journey of the Laws of Large Numbers

### The 18th Century: Bernoulli and de Moivre

In *Ars Conjectandi*, Jacob Bernoulli proved the law of large numbers: with enough trials, frequency approaches probability. It was the first rigorous limit theorem in probability. Soon after, de Moivre discovered the **normal distribution** (the bell curve), which Gauss would later put to work on measurement error.

### The 19th Century: Laplace's Central Limit Theorem

In *Théorie analytique des probabilités*, Laplace systematized the entire theory, proved a first form of the **central limit theorem**, and carried probability into astronomy, geodesy, and social statistics.

### The 20th Century: Rigorous Modern Proofs

- **Lyapunov and Lindeberg** supplied general conditions for the central limit theorem (independent but differently distributed variables included)
- **Kolmogorov** gave the law of large numbers a rigorous foundation in measure theory
- Probability rose from "limits by intuition" to "limit theorems with proof"

> From frequencies at the gambling table to the confidence intervals of statistical inference — the laws of large numbers have traveled three hundred years to become pillars of science.
