## The Story of Graph Theory

### 1736: Euler and the Königsberg Bridges

Euler proved the Königsberg problem had no solution, founding graph theory. His paper, "Solution of a Problem in the Geometry of Position," introduced the idea of "geometria situs" — geometry independent of distance and measurement. This is the shared origin of graph theory and topology.

### 19th Century: From Recreation to Discipline

- **Hamilton** posed the Hamiltonian circuit problem (1857): can you visit each vertex exactly once and return? It is the prototype of the traveling salesman problem
- **Cayley** studied tree structures, counting isomers of alkanes in chemistry — the first practical use of trees
- The **four color problem** was posed (1852), puzzling mathematicians for over a century

### Early 20th Century: The Golden Foundation

- **König's** *Theory of Finite and Infinite Graphs* (1936) made graph theory an independent discipline
- **Ramsey theory**: any sufficiently large graph must contain a certain structure (Ramsey numbers)
- **Graph theory meets algebra**: adjacency matrices, eigenvalues

### Mid-to-Late 20th Century: The Algorithmic Age

- **1956**: Dijkstra's shortest-path algorithm
- **1962**: Kruskal and Prim's minimum spanning tree algorithms
- **1965**: Dantzig's max-flow theory
- **1969**: The four color theorem computer-assisted proof (Appel & Haken) — the first famous proof depending on a computer

### Today: The Science of Networks

The internet, social networks, and biological networks gave birth to **network science**: small-world networks, scale-free networks, power-law distributions. Graph theory has evolved from a branch of mathematics into the core language for understanding the modern world.

> From a walk in Königsberg to analyzing the entire internet and social networks — graph theory turned "relations" themselves into an object of study.
