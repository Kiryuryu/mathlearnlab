## The Story of Probability

### 1654: The Betting Problem and the Birth of Probability

The French gambler Chevalier de Méré asked Pascal: if a game of chance is interrupted, how should the stake be fairly divided? Pascal corresponded with Fermat, and probability theory was born.

Their insight: **define chance through the equal likelihood of possible outcomes** — classical probability $P = \frac{\text{favorable outcomes}}{\text{total outcomes}}$.

### 18th Century: Bernoulli and the Normal Distribution

In *Ars Conjectandi*, Jacob Bernoulli proved the law of large numbers: with enough trials, frequency approaches probability. This was the first rigorous limit theorem of probability.

De Moivre then discovered the **normal distribution** (bell curve); Gauss later used it to handle measurement error.

### 19th Century: Laplace's Synthesis

In *Théorie Analytique des Probabilités*, Laplace systematized the whole theory, proved the **central limit theorem**, and applied probability to astronomy, measurement, and social statistics.

### 20th Century: Kolmogorov's Axiomatization

Probability was originally built on the intuition of "equal likelihood," which hit paradoxes when facing infinite spaces. In 1933, Kolmogorov axiomatized probability with **measure theory** — three axioms unifying every case. From then on, probability became a rigorous branch of mathematics.

### After the 20th Century: Fusion with Every Field

- **Information theory**: Shannon defined the amount of information using probability
- **Stochastic processes**: Markov chains, Brownian motion
- **Modern AI**: Bayesian inference, probabilistic graphical models, reinforcement learning

> From a question at the gambling table to the mathematical language of the deepest laws of the universe — probability has traveled more than three hundred years.
