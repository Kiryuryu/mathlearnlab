## The Beauty of Probability

### Certainty Hidden Inside Randomness

Flip a coin 100 times and you will not get exactly 50 heads. Flip it a million times and the proportion approaches 0.5 without bound. **Chaotic single events reveal precise laws under massive repetition** — the most beautiful paradox of probability.

$$\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{X_1+\cdots+X_n}{n} = \mu$$

### The Normal Distribution Is Everywhere

Why do height, weight, measurement error, and exam scores all form bell curves? Because they are **sums of countless tiny independent factors** — the central limit theorem guarantees such sums converge to the normal distribution. **A simple cause, a universal conclusion.**

$$f(x) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^2}}e^{-\frac{(x-\mu)^2}{2\sigma^2}}$$

### Bayesian Self-Correction

$$P(A|B) = \frac{P(B|A)\,P(A)}{P(B)}$$

The beauty of Bayes' theorem: it teaches us to **update beliefs with new evidence**. Prior + evidence = posterior. This is not merely a formula but an epistemology — science itself is a continuous process of updating beliefs with evidence.

### An Unexpected Meeting of e and π

The normal distribution formula contains both $e$ and $\pi$ — two seemingly unrelated constants meeting in a formula about "randomness." The Gaussian integral $\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{-x^2}dx = \sqrt{\pi}$ connects them, a classic example of "accidental beauty" in mathematics.

### Intuition Revolted

- **Birthday paradox**: with just 23 people there is a 50% chance two share a birthday
- **Monty Hall problem**: switching doors raises the win rate from 1/3 to 2/3
- **Test paradox**: with a 98%-accurate test, a positive result means only 50% probability of disease

These counterintuitive results remind us: **don't trust intuition — trust computation.**
