## The Beauty of Vectors

### From an Arrow to a World

An arrow pointing at a point — a vector is one of the simplest objects in mathematics. Yet with just two operations, addition and scalar multiplication, this simplest object spans an entire space. **The simplest object × simple operations = the most expansive structures.**

### Linear Combination: The Art of the Puzzle

$$\mathbf{v} = x\mathbf{i} + y\mathbf{j}$$

Any vector can be assembled from basis vectors — just as any color can be mixed from three primaries. This idea of "combining a few basic elements into everything" runs through all of linear algebra.

The standard basis is only the simplest choice; pick a different basis, and the same space takes on a new personality.

### The Inner Product: The Angle Between Directions

$$\mathbf{u} \cdot \mathbf{v} = \|\mathbf{u}\|\|\mathbf{v}\|\cos\theta$$

The inner product folds a "sense of direction" into algebra: orthogonal ⟺ inner product 0 ⟺ angle 90°. **Expressing geometry through numbers** — this is the most elegant thing about vectors.

### The Intuition of Dimension

A point in an $n$-dimensional vector space has $n$ coordinates — but that is only "the number of basis vectors."

Change the basis, and the same space wears a different face. **Space itself does not depend on coordinates; coordinates are merely its clothing.**

> The beauty of vectors is that they use the simplest language to speak both the intuition of geometry and the rigor of algebra.
