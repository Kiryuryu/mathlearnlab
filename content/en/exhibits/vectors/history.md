## The Story of Vectors

Every arrow, every coordinate grid, every list of numbers in a spreadsheet — each is a vector in disguise. The history of the vector is the story of how a simple "arrow" slowly grew into the universal language of modern science.

### 3rd Century BCE: The Seeds of Linear Systems

Ancient Babylon and China's *Nine Chapters on the Mathematical Art* could already solve systems of linear equations.

China's "method of equations" was essentially a systematic elimination using coefficient tables (matrices) — the ideas of vectors and matrices were planted in ancient times, waiting thousands of years to sprout.

### 17th Century: Analytic Geometry and Coordinates

Descartes translated geometry into algebra through coordinates — from then on a point carried numbers (the prototype of the vector). Leibniz began studying a "general theory of linear systems." Geometry and algebra, once strangers, began to speak the same language.

### 19th Century: The Birth of Vector Spaces

- **Hamilton** introduced quaternions, using "one real part plus three imaginary parts" to represent rotations in three-dimensional space — the vector, as a "quantity with direction," formally entered mathematics
- **Grassmann** built the axiomatic theory of $n$-dimensional vector spaces, elevating the vector from "arrow" to "abstract object"
- **Cayley** proposed the concept of a "linear transformation," throwing a bridge between vectors and matrices

### 20th Century: The Popularization of Vector Spaces

Functional analysis pushed vector spaces to infinite dimensions, making them the foundation of modern analysis and of quantum mechanics (the space of states). The same structure that once described a single arrow now holds the state of a quantum particle or the features of a photograph.

From forces in physics to features in data, the vector space became the universal language of modern science.

> From the method of equations in the *Nine Chapters* to infinite-dimensional function spaces — the abstraction of the "arrow" has walked more than two thousand years.
