## The Story of Bayes' Theorem

### The 18th Century: Bayes' Theorem

In 1763 (after his death), Thomas Bayes published a paper posing the problem of "inverse probability": knowing the outcome, how do we reason back to the cause? He gave the first sketch of today's Bayes' theorem. Laplace independently discovered the same formula and developed it into a systematic tool.

### The 19th Century: Bayes in Exile

The frequentist school gradually came to dominate, insisting that probability could only be an objective quantity such as "long-run frequency." Bayesian methods, controversial for their "subjective priors," retreated into the background.

### The 20th Century: The Bayesian Revival

- **Jeffreys and Savage** built the philosophical and theoretical foundations of Bayesian methods
- **The 1970s**: MCMC (Markov chain Monte Carlo) made complex Bayesian computation possible
- Computers turned "enumerating the posterior" into reality, and Bayesian methods returned to center stage

### Today: Bayes and AI

Naive Bayes, Bayesian networks, probabilistic graphical models, Bayesian optimization — much of modern machine learning rests on Bayesian updating.

> From Bayes' "inverse probability" to the belief updating of AI — one formula traveled two hundred years to become the core of intelligence.
