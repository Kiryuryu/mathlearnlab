## The Story of Probability Spaces

### 1654: The Problem of Points and the Birth of Probability

The French gambler Chevalier de Méré asked Pascal: when a two-player game is interrupted, how should the stakes be divided fairly? Pascal and Fermat settled the question through correspondence — and launched probability theory.

Their insight: **define chance through the equal likelihood of possible outcomes** — this is classical probability $P = \frac{\text{favorable outcomes}}{\text{total outcomes}}$.

### The 18th Century: Expectation and Bernoulli

- **Huygens** first systematized the concept of expectation
- **Bernoulli** proved the law of large numbers in *Ars Conjectandi*

### The 19th Century: Laplace's Synthesis

Laplace systematized the whole theory and applied probability to astronomy, measurement, and social statistics.

### The 20th Century: Kolmogorov's Axiomatization

Probability began on the intuition of "equally likely" outcomes, but infinite spaces produced paradoxes. In 1933, Kolmogorov axiomatized probability with **measure theory** — three axioms (non-negativity, normalization, additivity) unified every case. **From that moment, the probability space became a rigorous mathematical object.**

> From a question at the gambling table to the axioms of measure theory — it took three hundred years for the probability space to become rigorous.
