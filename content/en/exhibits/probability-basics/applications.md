## Probability Spaces in Action

### 1. Actuarial Science

Insurance companies price policies from **expected loss**: premium = probability of accident × average loss + operating costs.

$$E[\text{loss}] = \sum \text{loss}_i \times P(\text{loss}_i)$$

The probability space quantifies "risk" into a computable expectation — the very core of the insurance industry.

### 2. Pricing Financial Derivatives

The Black-Scholes option pricing formula rests on the **random walk** (Brownian motion) assumption: stock prices follow geometric Brownian motion, and an option's price is the discounted value of an expectation. Probability spaces are the foundation of quantitative finance.

### 3. Engineering Reliability

Engineers assess how long a system will work using **lifetime distributions** (exponential, Weibull). Given the lifetime distribution, one can compute "the probability that the system survives t years" — probability spaces make reliability designable.

### 4. Decision Theory and Games

- Expected utility theory: rational decision-making is maximizing expected utility
- Mixed strategies in game theory: randomization keeps your opponent guessing

### 5. Queueing Theory

Bank counters, servers, toll booths — all are queueing systems. Model the distributions of arrival intervals and service times with probability spaces, and you can compute the average waiting time — the foundation of operations research.

---

**Behind all these applications:** the probability space turns "uncertainty" into a computable mathematical object — the starting point of every modern decision built on risk and randomness.
