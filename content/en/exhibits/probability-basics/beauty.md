## The Beauty of Probability Spaces

### The Axiomatization of Randomness

Three axioms — non-negativity, normalization, additivity — define the entire probability space. **Three axioms unify every random phenomenon from dice rolls to quantum physics**, the finest demonstration of the power of mathematical axiomatization.

### Conditional Probability: Information Is Probability

$$P(A|B) = \frac{P(A \cap B)}{P(B)}$$

One formula embeds "knowledge" into the calculation of probability. The more you know, the sharper your judgment — conditional probability is the mathematical expression of "information."

### Expectation: The Scale Within Randomness

$$E[X] = \sum x \cdot P(X=x)$$

A single outcome cannot be predicted, but expectation acts like a scale, giving an accurate reading of the "long-run average." **A fixed point of certainty inside a random world.**

### The Elegance of Independence

$$P(A \cap B) = P(A) \cdot P(B)$$

Independence means "no interference" — the simplest multiplicative relationship. The entire law of large numbers and central limit theorem grow out of this humble condition.

> The beauty of the probability space lies in this: with the fewest possible axioms, it turns "uncertainty" into "computability."
