## The Story of Number Theory

### 3rd Century BCE: Euclid and Infinity

The *Elements* not only proves there are infinitely many primes, but also the Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic (unique factorization) — the two foundation stones of number theory for two millennia. Euclid's proof that primes are infinite is still cited among the most elegant proofs in mathematics.

### 17th Century: Fermat's Golden Age

Fermat — a judge by day, a mathematician by night — left behind a collection of the most beautiful propositions: **Fermat's little theorem**, **Fermat's Last Theorem** ($x^n+y^n=z^n$ has no integer solutions). His marginal note "I have discovered a truly marvelous proof, which this margin is too narrow to contain" baffled mathematicians for 358 years until Wiles proved it in 1994.

### 18th Century: Euler's Dance of Formulas

Euler proved the generalization of Fermat's little theorem (Euler's theorem), invented the $\varphi$ function (Euler's totient), and proved the connection between the harmonic series and primes. He also discovered the famous Euler product formula, mysteriously linking infinite series to primes.

### 19th Century: Gauss and Riemann

- **Gauss's** *Disquisitiones Arithmeticae* (1801) founded modern number theory, including the proof of quadratic reciprocity (the "queen of number theory"); he guessed the prime number theorem at age 15
- **Riemann**, in an 8-page paper in 1859, proposed the Riemann $\zeta$ function and the Riemann hypothesis — from then on, the distribution of primes became deeply entwined with complex analysis

### 20th Century to Today: From Pure Mathematics to Cryptography

- **1976**: Diffie–Hellman key exchange — number theory leapt from "pure mathematics" to the bedrock of network security
- **1977**: RSA encryption published; the hardness of factoring became a pillar of modern cryptography
- **1994**: Wiles proved Fermat's Last Theorem, fulfilling a 358-year-old dream with modern tools
- **2024**: GIMPS distributed computing found a Mersenne prime exceeding 100 million digits

> From Euclid's *Elements* to the RSA guarding the internet — number theory traveled two thousand years, from "pure beauty" to "practical beauty."
