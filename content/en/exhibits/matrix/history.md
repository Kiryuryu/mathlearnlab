## The Story of Matrices

### 18th Century: The Birth of Determinants

The Japanese mathematician Seki Kowa and Leibniz studied determinants almost simultaneously.

In 1750 Cramer gave a formula for solving linear systems with determinants (Cramer's rule) — notably, the determinant appeared before the matrix itself.

### 19th Century: Matrices Step Onstage

- **Cayley** formally introduced the concept of the "matrix" and matrix multiplication in 1858, systematically algebraizing linear transformations
- **Sylvester** coined the term "matrix" and studied the invariants of matrices

From then on the matrix rose from "a trick for solving systems of equations" to "the language for studying linear transformations."

A matrix was no longer just a way to write equations — it was a way to see transformations as objects that could themselves be added, multiplied, and studied.

### 20th Century: Matrices and the Computer

- **Turing and von Neumann** made heavy use of matrices in early computer design
- **Numerical linear algebra** developed stable matrix algorithms (LU decomposition, QR decomposition)
- **The data age**: matrices became the common language of machine learning, image processing, and scientific computing

In a GPU, a single frame is the product of tens of millions of matrix multiplications — the abstract idea of a "deformer of space" now runs the entire digital world. Every search, every photograph, every neural network runs on matrices.

> From Cramer's rule to the matrix multiplications inside a GPU — the "deformer of space" rules modern computation.
