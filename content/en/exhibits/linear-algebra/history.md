## The Story of Linear Algebra

### 3rd Century BCE: The Seeds of Linear Systems

Babylon and China's *Nine Chapters on the Mathematical Art* could already solve systems of linear equations. China's "method of equations" was essentially a prototype of **Gaussian elimination** — systematically eliminating unknowns using coefficient tables (matrices).

### 17th Century: Analytic Geometry and Vectors

Descartes translated geometry into algebra through coordinates, paving the way for vectors. Leibniz began studying a "general theory of linear systems."

### 18th Century: The Birth of Determinants

The Japanese mathematician Seki Kowa and Leibniz studied determinants almost simultaneously. In 1750 Cramer gave the formula for solving linear systems with determinants (Cramer's rule).

### 19th Century: Matrices and Abstraction

- **Cayley** formally introduced the concept of a "matrix" and matrix multiplication in 1858, systematically algebraizing linear transformations
- **Hamilton** put forward the idea of vector spaces
- **Grassmann** built the axiomatic theory of n-dimensional vector spaces

Linear algebra rose from a "technique for solving equations" to "the discipline of studying vector spaces."

### 20th Century: Fusion with Every Field

- **Quantum mechanics**: Heisenberg expressed physical quantities as matrices — matrix mechanics
- **Functional analysis**: infinite-dimensional vector spaces became the foundation of analysis
- **The data age**: PCA, singular value decomposition, and neural-network backpropagation all rest on linear algebra

> From the method of equations in the *Nine Chapters* to the mathematics ruling modern data science — linear algebra is the "algebra of space."
